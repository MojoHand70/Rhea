# Nobody Occupies Rhea's Combined Ground Yet

As of October 2026, no product, open-source project, startup, or research system combines Rhea's full architecture: an AI that authors business rules as versioned data, a recorded draft → approved → active → superseded lifecycle, a deterministic kernel executing those rules over an append-only event log, wipe-and-replay reproducing identical state, and per-field provenance `(event_id, rule_id, rule_version)`. Every ingredient exists somewhere in production — but always in a different system, and never welded together. The closest single neighbor is **GoRules** (rules as versioned JSON, AI copilot drafts, human-gated release, deterministic engine — but no event log, no state, no replay). The closest ERP-domain neighbors are **Lensing** (AI authors business logic from plain language with sandbox-then-approve) and the open-source **Accounted** (agent proposes, human approves, append-only vouchers — but AI operates rather than authors). The closest architectural statements are 2026 agent-runtime research papers (**ESAA**, **ActiveGraph**) that apply the append-only-log/deterministic-replay/lineage kernel to *agent execution* instead of business rules. What remains genuinely unoccupied is Rhea's downstream half: rules that don't just *decide* but *derive durable state*, with every derived field carrying its causal provenance triple and the whole state checkable by replay — plus the approval itself recorded as an event in the same log. No commercial AI-native ERP, rules engine, ledger API, or research prototype found makes that combination, and the funded ERP category competes on automation outcomes rather than architectural guarantees, leaving Rhea's demonstrable (not merely claimable) auditability as its sharpest differentiation.

## AI-native ERPs put the AI at the transaction layer, never the rule layer

The funded AI-native ERP cohort — roughly half a billion dollars of venture money in just over a year ([ERP Research](https://www.erpresearch.com/en-us/ai-native-erp)) — has converged on one pattern: **AI drafts transactions, humans approve, an "immutable audit trail" records it**. That is Rhea's human-in-the-loop invariant applied one layer too low. **Rillet** ($100M Series C at a $1B valuation, 600+ customers) pitches "accounting superintelligence with AI agents doing finance work inside a real-time general ledger, with human approval and a full audit trail" ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/rillet-100m-series-c-1bn-accounting-ai); [Rillet blog](https://www.rillet.com/blog/rillet-raises-100m-series-c-at-1b-valuation-to-build-accounting-superintelligence)) — but the ledger logic is vendor-coded and no rules-as-data, replay, or determinism story appears anywhere in its public material. **DualEntry** ($90M Series A) claims "immutable audit trails with multi-step approvals" on a fast-migration product whose business logic is vendor-configured, not runtime-definable ([RoboCFO landscape](https://robocfo.ai/frameworks/ai-native-erp-landscape)). **Campfire** ($65M Series B) and **Digits** push the logic into proprietary ML model weights — a "Large Accounting Model" and "non-generative predictive AI models" respectively ([ERPClaw comparison](https://www.erpclaw.ai/blog/5-ai-native-erps-that-earn-the-label/); [Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/digits-announces-autonomous-general-ledger)) — the exact opposite of Rhea's inspectable versioned rules.

Two vendors come closer in spirit. **Light** (€25M, Copenhagen) is the strongest *philosophical* match: its "organic software" manifesto declares "every correction is training… the system takes that as instruction" and "reshapes itself around the business as it is today" ([Light manifesto](https://light.inc/manifesto)) — runtime-shaped behavior under approval policies. But the learning is implicit model adaptation, never an inspectable versioned rule artifact, with no lifecycle, replay, or provenance anywhere in its material. **Lensing** (ex-Everest Systems, $140M from Sutter Hill and others, founded by SAP HANA architect Franz Faerber) is the closest commercial analog on the rule-authoring axis: its AiSpecify lets business users "build entire applications, specification to launch… in plain English," with a "Live Sandbox to simulate any changes before approving them" ([lensing.ai](https://lensing.ai/)) — structurally parallel to Rhea's draft → simulate → approve → active. The difference: the artifact is application configuration on a metadata platform, not strict-JSON rules executed by a deterministic kernel over an event log, and Lensing's pages "don't specify detailed versioning, audit trails, or replay mechanisms for configurations" ([lensing.ai](https://lensing.ai/)).

The incumbents mirror the startups. SAP's **Joule Studio** agent builder (GA Q1 2026) lets enterprises "define goals, permissions, triggers, and workflows using low-code and no-code capabilities" ([AIMultiple](https://aimultiple.com/sap-ai-agents)) — but humans author with AI assistance, inverted from Rhea where AI authors and humans approve, and the agents orchestrate over fixed vendor-coded transactional logic. **NetSuite Next** adds agentic workflows that "can be approved step by step or allowed to act on their own," while remaining "still forms-based underneath" ([ERPClaw](https://www.erpclaw.ai/blog/5-ai-native-erps-that-earn-the-label/)). Critically, "immutable audit trail" across this whole category means a log *about* mutable state, not an event log *from which* state is derived. That inversion — state as a pure function of log plus versioned rules — has no commercial occupant in the ERP category.

## GoRules is the closest single neighbor, and it stops exactly where Rhea's kernel begins

The decision-management industry independently converged in 2025–2026 on Rhea's authoring loop, which means the *loop itself* is no longer unique — and that is useful validation. **GoRules** is the most complete instance anywhere: its open-source ZEN engine (Rust, MIT, ~2,000 GitHub stars) stores business logic as portable JSON Decision Models that "evaluate in microseconds, run identically on every platform" ([gorules/zen](https://github.com/gorules/zen)), while the commercial layer adds an AI copilot that drafts and modifies decision graphs from natural-language policy or PDFs, shows the proposal **as a diff the user accepts or rejects**, auto-generates scenario tests that run "before anything reaches a reviewer," and keeps releasing "with your reviewers — the same approvals and audit trail as any human change" ([GoRules AI](https://gorules.io/ai); [GoRules architecture](https://docs.gorules.io/developers/overview/architecture)). That is Rhea's invariants 2 and 3 as a shipping product. The sharpest difference: ZEN is a stateless request/response decision evaluator. There is no append-only business event log, no replay that reproduces object state through historical rule versions, and no per-derived-field provenance — the audit trail covers *rule changes*, not *derived facts*. GoRules evaluates decisions; Rhea's kernel builds state from them.

The rest of the category fills in the pattern's edges. IBM ODM's "Bob mode" (June 2026) generates complete rule projects from a single policy document — but the output includes a Java domain model (code, not pure data) and lands in a developer IDE with no described approval gate ([IBM Community blog](https://community.ibm.com/community/user/blogs/mathias-mouly1/2026/06/08/odm-rule-designer-bob-mode)). DecisionRules' Gemini-backed assistant builds decision tables from plain language with "your team stays in control" rhetoric but no documented lifecycle ([DecisionRules](https://www.decisionrules.io/en/articles/ai-for-business-rules-from-plain-language-to-working-logic/)). **Camunda** frames the market's dominant alternative explicitly: choose between "deterministic logic using DMN decision tables, or invoking AI agents via connectors" at runtime ([Camunda blog](https://camunda.com/blog/2025/07/ai-agent-or-based-rule-dmn-ai-powered-orchestration/)) — the inverse of Rhea, which puts the LLM at authoring time only. On the formal-methods side, the **Cedar** research line (AutoCedar, autoformalization pipelines) has LLMs synthesize deterministic authorization policies under verifier guidance ([arXiv 2607.03656](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.03656v1); [arXiv 2606.26649](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26649)) — machine verification standing in where Rhea puts a human approver, in a domain (allow/deny) where nothing is derived. And in Rhea's own statutory territory, **Avalara** now lets users "create, edit, and visualize complex compliance rules using simple AI prompts" ([Avalara blog](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2025/09/avalara-introduces-agentic-ai.html)) and **Fonoa** articulates the same three-part split — AI monitors and proposes, humans decide, a deterministic tax engine executes ([Fonoa](https://www.fonoa.com/resources/blog/where-ai-belongs-in-your-tax-stack)) — but neither publishes a formal version lifecycle for AI-drafted rules, and neither derives tenant state from an event log.

## The event-sourcing and ledger worlds have Rhea's kernel but nobody's rule layer

The log-and-replay half of Rhea is mature infrastructure — and uniformly lacks runtime-definable, AI-authored logic. **Kurrent** (ex-EventStore) sells "the only database purpose-built for event sourcing with native immutability, global ordering, real-time subscriptions, and projections" and pivoted in 2026 to agentic AI — but as a substrate *for* agents (observability, "compliance trails to deterministic recovery"), not as an executor of AI-written rules; projections remain ordinary developer code, and no ERP positioning appears anywhere ([KurrentDB](https://kurrentdb.kurrent.io/event-sourcing/); [Kurrent events](https://www.kurrent.io/events/)). Durable-execution platforms (Temporal, DBOS, Restate, Golem) make determinism a *recovery* mechanism for developer-written workflows — DBOS "replays from the last committed step" and requires workflow bodies to be deterministic ([DBOS blog](https://www.dbos.dev/blog/what-is-lightweight-durable-execution)); Temporal ships versioning/patching precisely to *escape* strict replay when code changes ([Temporal docs](https://docs.temporal.io/develop/go/versioning)) — the opposite of Rhea's replay-as-proof. Bitemporal databases get closest on auditability: **XTDB** promises to "demonstrate what data you actually had available when a decision was made" ([XTDB FAQ](https://v1-docs.xtdb.com/resources/faq/)) — but its provenance is *temporal* (which fact, when), not *causal* (which event and rule version produced this field); nothing is derived, so there is no analog of "nothing appears in state without a rule."

Ledger infrastructure is the one category already shipping "logic as reviewed, versioned data." **Formance** is the closest commercial analog to Rhea's rule layer: Numscript is a money-movement DSL with "one reviewed, versioned script per business event" as recommended practice, and its 2026 agentic architecture blocks agent write access, gates AI-proposed commands behind "signed human approval," and stamps every proposal with agent ID, model version, and mandate ID ([Formance](https://www.formance.com/blog/embedded-finance/ai-payments-agentic-ledger-stack); [Numscript](https://www.formance.com/platform/numscript)). But humans author the scripts; the AI only proposes transaction *instances* within them, and the ledger is the record, not a replayable projection. **Fragment** versions declarative ledger schemas and markets to coding agents ([fragment.dev](https://fragment.dev/)); **TigerBeetle** enforces double-entry invariants inside the database itself ([TigerBeetle DESIGN.md](https://github.com/tigerbeetle/tigerbeetle/blob/main/docs/DESIGN.md)) — a kernel whose rule set is deliberately frozen at compile time, the exact opposite trade from Rhea's runtime extensibility.

The nearest *architectural statements* to Rhea's kernel come from 2026 agent-runtime research. **ESAA** ("Event Sourcing for Autonomous Agents") has agents "emit structured intentions while a deterministic orchestrator validates, persists events in an append-only log, and projects a verifiable materialized view" ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/401278725_ESAA_Event_Sourcing_for_Autonomous_Agents_in_LLM-Based_Software_Engineering)). **ActiveGraph** ("The Log is the Agent," arXiv May 2026) makes "the append-only event log the source of truth" with deterministic replay and "end-to-end lineage… down to the individual model call that produced each artifact" ([arXiv 2605.21997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21997)). Both apply the pattern to agent execution, keeping the LLM *inside* the replayed loop — and the field has already named the resulting failure mode: the "Replay Divergence Problem," where model-version or prompt changes break event-sourcing audit assumptions ([Zylos](https://zylos.ai/research/2026-04-26-replayable-agent-runtimes/)). Rhea sidesteps this by construction: the LLM authors rules offline and never appears in the execution path, so the kernel contains no model call to diverge.

## Open source and research supply fragments; the fusion is unclaimed

The 2025–2026 open-source AI-ERP cohort converges on the same architecture as the funded startups: deterministic hand-written tool layer, LLM as operator, human approval of transactions. **Accounted** (398 stars, AGPL) is the most mature and philosophically closest on invariants — 150+ MCP tools so "an AI agent can do the books," posting "staged for human approval, so the agent proposes and you decide," voucher immutability and period locks "enforced by database triggers, not by convention" ([erp-mafia/accounted](https://github.com/erp-mafia/accounted)) — but its logic is TypeScript plus triggers, never AI-authored data, with no rule versioning, replay claim, or field provenance. **ERPClaw** adds a hash-chained append-only ledger, reversal-only corrections, and AI-generated modules that deploy only after rule-checks plus explicit approval ([erpclaw.ai](https://www.erpclaw.ai/blog/5-ai-native-erps-that-earn-the-label/)) — but the AI generates *code modules*, not rules-as-data. **AiNativeERP** has an explicit "LLM generates candidate intent → validate → draft → user confirms → execute" pipeline ([Edison0621/AiNativeERP](https://github.com/Edison0621/AiNativeERP)) — governing intents, not rules. The lone "ERP as a language for LLMs" claim, **erp-dsl**, is an obscure repo with no kernel, events, or traction ([jjaim519/erp-dsl](https://github.com/jjaim519/erp-dsl)).

Research anticipates Rhea piecewise. The LLM→DSL→deterministic-engine split is now recognized best practice — Catala tax-law generation benchmarks ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.nllp-1.4/)), LLM-generated DMN from Dutch legislation with expert verification ([arXiv 2604.17153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17153)), and Terpsichora's schema-validated JSON declarative process models — nearly Rhea's agent contract verbatim, though for synthetic dataset generation with automated rather than human validation ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44311-025-00029-1)). The Rules-as-Code community converged socially on Rhea's exact division of labor — Jason Morris's Blawx + GPT-4 experiments plus the PHAC Privacy Act demo combine AI-assisted authoring, human validation, and deterministic execution with reasoning traces linked to source text ([Blawx post](https://gauntlet173.github.io/post/2023_04_12_llm_and_rac/); [PHACDataHub/privacy_rac_demo](https://github.com/PHACDataHub/privacy_rac_demo)) — but delivers it as artisan git-repo workflow; Rhea mechanizes what RaC does socially. The April 2026 **Agentic BPM manifesto** restates Rhea's governance as a research program — "framed autonomy" with normative frames, human escalation, and mandatory traceability ([arXiv 2603.18916](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18916v3)) — while putting the agent *inside* the runtime, where Rhea pushes autonomy entirely into authoring. On lineage: Rhea's **REA** ancestry (McCarthy 1982, ISO/IEC 15944-4 with its business-rules repository) survives today only in the P2P-commons world (Valueflows, hREA on Holochain) ([valueflo.ws](https://www.valueflo.ws/); [hrea.io](https://hrea.io/)), and targeted searching surfaced **no REA + LLM work anywhere** ([ISO 15944-4](https://www.iso.org/standard/67199.html)) — that lineage is uncontested. Finally, the self-evolving-agent literature is the empirical case *for* Rhea's design: agents that modify themselves exhibit "misevolution" and non-monotonic forgetting ([arXiv 2509.26354](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.26354); [arXiv 2605.09315](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09315)), and no surveyed system expresses behavior change as versioned declarative data behind a human gate.

## The unoccupied ground, dimension by dimension

The map resolves cleanly into closest neighbors per axis, with the combination empty:

| Rhea dimension | Closest neighbor(s) | What's still missing there |
|---|---|---|
| AI authors rules as versioned data | **GoRules** (JSON rules, AI diff-review, human release); Lensing AiSpecify; IBM Bob mode | No event log, no state derivation; Lensing's artifact is platform config; IBM's is part code |
| Draft → approved → active lifecycle | GoRules releases; Copilot Studio admin approval; ERPClaw deploy-on-approval | Nowhere a first-class state machine on rule versions; Copilot Studio re-publishes logic *without* re-approval; no one records the approval as an event in the execution log |
| Deterministic kernel over append-only log | Kurrent/Axon (infrastructure); ESAA, ActiveGraph (agent research); ERPClaw's hash-chained GL | Logic over the log is developer code or the LLM itself; no rules-as-data layer |
| Replay reproduces identical state | DBOS/Temporal (replay for *recovery*); event-sourcing pattern literature | Replay as *proof* of business state through historical rule versions exists nowhere as a product |
| Per-field provenance (event, rule, version) | XTDB (temporal provenance); Formance (per-transaction agent/model/mandate stamps); Catala (statute-text pairing) | All are provenance of *recorded* facts or *actions*; causal provenance of *derived* fields has no occupant |
| Statutory domain as data packs | Fonoa/Avalara (vendor-maintained tax content); Odoo localizations | Shipped as vendor code/config, not swappable declarative packs over an event kernel |

Three structural findings sharpen the positioning. First, the field splits on *where the LLM sits*: agent runtimes make the LLM a durable executor and then fight the Replay Divergence Problem; decision platforms put it at authoring time but have no state; Rhea is the only found design where the LLM authors and is then excluded from execution entirely over a stateful kernel. Second, every competitor's explainability is *narrative* — the agent logs what it did — while Rhea's is *structural*: the state itself carries its derivation, and the claim is checkable by wiping projections and replaying. Buyer-side criteria are already drifting toward Rhea's language ("the ERP records which data was used, which model or policy was applied… and who approved it" — [erpclaw.ai](https://www.erpclaw.ai/ai-native-erp/)) without any vendor possessing the mechanism that makes it verifiable. Third, the approval-as-event detail is uniquely Rhea's: GoRules keeps an audit trail *beside* execution data, Copilot Studio an admin-console state — no surveyed system records the human approval as a replayable event in the same log the kernel executes.

Honest caveats: the internal architectures of Rillet, DualEntry, Campfire, and Light are not publicly documented, so "not event-sourced" is absence of evidence; a stealth startup with the full loop would not surface in public material; and Drools/Kogito, Pega, Trisotech, SAP Signavio, and the billing-engine vendors (Zuora, Metronome, Orb) went unexamined. None of these gaps, however, touches the central result — across roughly sixty systems surveyed in five domains, every one covers at most three of Rhea's six properties, and no two of them cover complementary halves in a single shipped system.

## Conclusion

The survey's most useful insight is that Rhea's novelty is not the authoring loop — "AI drafts, human approves, deterministic engine executes" commoditized across GoRules, IBM, DecisionRules, Avalara, and Formance during 2025–2026, which validates the pattern and removes it as a moat. The defensible ground is downstream: rules that derive durable state over an append-only log, with replay as proof rather than recovery, per-field causal provenance, and the approval itself living in the replayed log. That combination is absent from every commercial, open-source, and academic system found, and the incumbents' mutable-ORM and the startups' mutable-ledger architectures cannot retrofit it without a rewrite — the inversion (state as a function of log + rules) must be foundational.

Two tailwinds strengthen the position. The agent-runtime community's Replay Divergence Problem is independent confirmation that Rhea's "model output is data, never executed" invariant is the correct side of a fork the rest of the field is only now discovering it must take. And the self-evolution risk literature (misevolution, forgetting) is an empirical argument that behavior change in business systems *needs* versioned, human-gated, replayable rule artifacts — the field is writing Rhea's requirements document without building the system. The practical implication for positioning: lead with the demonstrable mechanism (wipe, replay, compare — live) rather than the audit-trail vocabulary every competitor already uses, because the vocabulary is saturated and the mechanism has no other owner.
