# PACKS.md — the target catalog of a big AIRP

A vision, not a plan of record (KK and Claude, 2026-10-23): the packs Rhea grows into so it stands
beside the big ERPs in breadth while being what none of them is. It builds on KK's lists —
`targetRhea.md` (sixteen domains, "for all the above we need Rhea Apps") and `tMVP.md` (the
editions and the standard apps) — and on the layers agreed the same day ("Core, market packs and
business packs", DIRECTION). Internal: it names competitors; a shareable version names them as
categories.

## The idea

A big ERP sells modules that consultants configure for months; a Polish small-business tool sells
an invoice book; an AI-native finance tool automates one department. Rhea's catalog is **packs of
Apps, installed per company and shaped by an interview**: the breadth of the first, the price and
ease of the second, the automation of the third — and what none of them has: a company's model
learned from its own evidence, automatic by default with trust earned step by step, every number
explained to its fact, rule and law, a network that learns across companies, and partners —
accounting offices first — who author and sell packs, so an office can implement "a big ERP" for
its clients (tMVP).

## The market, in two dimensions

Researched 2026-10-23: `reports/AI-native ERP newcomers 2026-10.md` and `reports/Incumbent ERP
vendors 2026-10.md` (coverage, segments, AI, price, Poland), beside the earlier architecture
survey `reports/Products similar to Rhea.md`. Vendor claims, sourced there.

**The newcomers** (Light, Rillet, Campfire, DualEntry, Numeric, Digits, Lensing; Pennylane in
Europe). They prove three things Rhea also bets on: a finance core can be replaced in weeks, not
months (4–16 weeks, vendor-led, often included in the price); agents that draft while a person
approves are what buyers now expect; audit and accounting firms are the channel (EY–Rillet,
KPMG–Light; Digits charges firms only for clients that ran untouched). Their limits are the
catalog's opening: **the finance office only** — no manufacturing anywhere, inventory only through
a partner (Doss), procurement stopping at the purchase order, no native payroll, CRM or projects;
**US GAAP first**; their "learning" lives in model weights, not in rules a person can read and
approve; **no Poland** — no KSeF, no JPK on any page found.

**Who attacks Europe** (KK's question). Of the cohort, two are European by birth: **Light**
(Copenhagen; 21 country pages — Peppol, XRechnung and ELSTER, SdI, SII, SAF-T, MTD — and no
Poland) and **Pennylane** (Paris; France, Germany, Spain; 6,500+ accounting firms claimed; free for
firms that keep their clients' books on it; certified for France's September 2026 e-invoicing
mandate). Two Americans came through **London** — **Campfire** (June 2026, UK partner firms) and
**DualEntry** (European headquarters, August 2026) — with no EU tax depth found. **Rillet,
Numeric, Digits and Lensing**: no European move found. Smaller European AI-accounting players are
appearing — **Integral** (Berlin, €18M, an AI-native accounting firm in Germany), **Finto**
(Munich, AI accounting agents, seed), **Aderis** (a European "FinTech ERP") — and **Charted**, an
American AP add-on, says its e-invoicing reaches Poland this quarter, the first sign of KSeF in the
cohort's orbit. Nobody native to Poland or Central Europe; Light is the one to watch, a country
page away. Europe is a market of mandates — France 2026, Germany 2027–2028, Poland's KSeF for every
VAT payer in 2026 and micro-firms from 1 January 2027 — and a mandate is the moment a company
changes software.

**The old guard** (SAP, Oracle and NetSuite, IFS, Microsoft, Infor, Epicor, Sage, Workday, Unit4,
Odoo, Acumatica). They own the breadth: every domain of this catalog, industry editions, partner
ecosystems, marketplaces (AppSource, SuiteApp, Odoo's 50,000 apps, Oracle's agent marketplace).
Their AI is an assistant and agents laid over transactional logic that does not change; each now
lets customers build agents (Joule Studio, a year late; Oracle AI Agent Studio; Copilot Studio;
Workday's Flowise); adoption is thin (3% of SAP customers use it productively, per DSAG); AI is
used to pull customers to the cloud. Their weaknesses: implementation (a median of nine months;
the licence a fifth of an Odoo budget, per a partner), price for small firms, and **Polish law
through partners or late** — Business Central through IT.integro, NetSuite through Staria and RSM,
IFS through Pagero, Dynamics 365 Finance's KSeF payment numbers in March 2027.

**The Polish incumbents** (Comarch — Optima's 70,000 firms, 52.7% of small firms per IDC; Symfonia,
enova365, InsERT, Asseco; wFirma, inFakt, Saldeo, mOrganizer online). The legal depth is theirs,
on older architecture (desktop lines, a "cloud" that is hosted desktop software), with AI in beta
(Comarch's ChatERP, Symfonia's chat). Accounting offices work across **three layers** — the books
per client (Optima, Rewizor, Symfonia, enova365, WAPRO), document capture (Saldeo, about 3,000
offices), the client portal or online service (wFirma, about 4,000 offices) — which Visma is
consolidating; no product found unites them on one ledger with AI that knows Polish law.

**What it changes in the catalog.**

1. *Wave 0 has a date:* KSeF reaches micro-firms on 1 January 2027 and its penalties start that
   year — every sole trader and their office changes how invoices flow. Rhea for Sole Proprietors
   and Rhea for Accounting Offices, with KPiR and ryczałt, are due for it.
2. *The office pack unites the three layers* — the books for every client, documents in (KSeF,
   mail, scans) and the client's own window — on one ledger. That is the gap nobody fills.
3. *Free for firms is proven* (Pennylane, Digits' firm pricing): the office pack stays free; paying
   by outcome — clients that ran untouched — fits the learning brief's measure of an accountant's
   touches per document.
4. *Finance parity first:* the newcomers' core — AP automation, AR and collections, the close
   without hands — belongs in waves 0–1, because that is what any buyer now compares.
5. *Apps, not agents:* everyone lets customers build agents over logic that does not change; Rhea's
   interview writes the logic itself, as approved, versioned, explained data. Say it that way.
6. *Breadth is where the newcomers stop:* Sales, Purchasing, WMS, Production, HR and payroll,
   Projects — the domain packs of waves 1–2 are where Rhea leaves the cohort behind while staying
   lighter than the old guard.
7. *Germany when its mandate bites* (issuing from 2027–2028), before Light adds Poland — the
   cross-border pair is where a European AIRP from Poland is native and the others are not.

## The six shelves

1. **Core** — Rhea with nothing installed. Free everywhere.
2. **Market packs** — the law, per country: a base, one regime per company, modules.
3. **Domain packs** — the functions of a company, country-neutral (KK's sixteen, and the gaps).
4. **Business packs** — a business model: the domain packs' Apps chosen and shaped, and its own.
5. **Connector packs** — the world outside: banks, e-invoicing, the state, marketplaces, shops,
   payments, carriers, cash registers, mail.
6. **Intelligence packs** — what the network learns: forecasting, anomalies, the close without
   hands, audit readiness, advice.

Domain packs lean on the core and, where the law touches them, on the market's modules (HR on
payroll, Sales on the invoice); business packs are selections; connectors bring facts in through
channels; intelligence packs read and propose — they never write.

## 1. Core

The five things that never change — ledger, account, journal, balance, accounting period — and

"why?", search, the interview and the App designer, Ustawienia → Pakiety.

## 6. Intelligence packs

The close without hands (the month closed, the JPK sent, the client told what to pay); cash-flow
forecast; anomalies and fraud (a duplicate invoice, an unusual payment, an account off the white
list); audit readiness; the regime advisor for sole traders (skala, liniowy or ryczałt, from their
own numbers — a strong first hook); collections and pricing advice; benchmarks from the network,
anonymous and only where the companies agreed (the privacy rules of 2026-10-18 apply).

## Waves

Aligned with the growth plan: the demo in April 2027, the big picture in October 2027.

- **Wave 0 — the first strangers (now to early 2027).** The core with Rachunki i karty;
  installation per company; Poland split into Podstawa, Pełna księgowość and Środki trwałe; KPiR;
  Ewidencja przychodów; Rhea for Sole Proprietors and for Accounting Offices; parity with wFirma,
  inFakt, Saldeo and mOrganizer; banks, KSeF, GUS, JPK sending, migration, mail-in.
- **Wave 1 — the demo (April 2027).** Kadry i płace and HR; Sales and orders; Purchasing; CRM;
  Case; Time and projects; Documents and contracts; Fleet; Treasury and Controlling basics;
  Rhea for eCommerce, SaaS and Professional Services; marketplaces and payments.
- **Wave 2 — the big picture (October 2027).** WMS; Production; Subscription in depth;
  Multicompany and consolidation; Investments; Logistics; Audit readiness; Germany and the EU
  layer; Rhea for Holdings, Construction, Real Estate, NGOs, Wholesale; the partner marketplace
  opens.
- **Wave 3 — 2028.** Plant, Quality, Field service, POS and retail, ESG; more countries;
  intelligence at the network's scale.

## What makes it competitive

- **The breadth of a big ERP, bought one pack at a time** — nobody buys "the ERP"; a company
  installs what its business needs, and Rhea proposes it from evidence.
- **Implemented by interview** — done when nothing is left unexplained, not when a budget is.
- **Automatic by default, trust earned**, and every number explained to its law.
- **The law done right where it is local** — KSeF, JPK, GUS — where global ERPs are thin, and well
  past the invoice book where the local tools stop.
- **The network learns** — a lesson many companies hold becomes Rhea's; offices teach many
  clients at once.
- **Partners author and sell packs** — the free floor for sole traders and offices is the channel;
  the marketplace is how the catalog grows faster than Rhea's own team.
