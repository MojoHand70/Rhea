## Q1. What Rhea IS, what it is NOT, who it is for, and what "AiRP" and the seven eligibility rules say

### Takeaway
Rhea is an experimental, AI-authored, deterministic-kernel ERP *system of record* (not a layer on top of an ERP): facts live in an append-only event log, business logic is versioned rule data drafted by an LLM and activated only by human approval, and all state is a replayable projection with per-field provenance. It is explicitly not a product, has no customers, no auth, no multi-tenant, no performance work; its stated audience is "thousands of smaller and bigger EU businesses", beachhead Poland, with the owner/accountant as the human in the loop. "AiRP" (AI Resource Planning) is the brand for "AI authors semantics, never executes"; the seven eligibility rules map each ingredient of a big ERP to a Rhea mechanism.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Rhea positions itself as a *replacement* for the ERP's transactional core (it is the ledger and the system of record), whereas a close platform is an *overlay* that reads an existing GL. This is the single most important structural fact for a comparison with Numeric: the two do not sit in the same layer.
- Every "guarantee" Rhea cares about is architectural (append-only, replay, provenance, containment) rather than functional (features for a finance team). The documents consistently describe outcomes for *auditability* and *implementation speed*, not for *close-cycle time*.

### Gaps
- No document defines a target company size, headcount, or revenue band more precisely than "smaller and bigger EU businesses" / "Polish SME".
- No persona document for a controller, close manager or auditor as *user*; the accountant appears as approver and as future language steward only.

## Q3. Which of Rhea's claims are architectural guarantees (tested invariants) vs. aspirations

### Inferences
- Numeric and Rhea answer different questions ("how do I close faster on the ERP I have" vs. "what if the ERP itself were a replayable, AI-authored rule corpus"), so Numeric does not make Rhea *pointless*; but Numeric does occupy the *buyer attention* (CFO, close pain, measurable ROI) that Rhea has not yet addressed at all, and it does so with real customers, real ERP connectors and a priced product.
- The most credible rebuttal available from Rhea's own material is structural: an overlay inherits the reconciliation problem from mutable ledgers, while Rhea's single-root-fact derivation removes one class of it (intercompany) by construction and can turn bank matching into auditable rules — but that rebuttal is today a specification plus one test, not a product.
- The honest comparative framing the owner's own documents support: Rhea's defensible claims are the six architectural dimensions; Rhea's *risks* are everything a close platform already has (connectors, workflow, buyers, revenue).
