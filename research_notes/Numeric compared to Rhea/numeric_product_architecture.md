# Numeric (numeric.io) — product scope and architecture, as of 2026-10-08

Scope note: Numeric Technologies, Inc., San Francisco (also NY and London offices), founded 2020 by Parker Gilbert (CEO), Anthony Alvernaz and Andrew Bihl; $89M raised (Seed $10M May 2024, Series A $28M Oct 2024 led by Menlo, Series B $51M Nov 2025 led by IVP). Every claim below is dated. Anything sourced before 2025-01-01 is flagged "(pre-2025)". Almost all primary material is the vendor's own marketing, help-center and press release text; independent verification is thin and is called out where it exists. The single most important fact for a Rhea comparison: on 2026-09-22 Numeric launched a second product line, the "Financial Data Platform" (FDP), which it markets as "ERP Replacement" — an event-store with derived accounting — alongside its original close-management layer that sits on top of an external ERP. Both lines are sold concurrently.

## Key Question 1 — Full module inventory (what each module concretely does)

### Takeaway
As of October 2026 Numeric sells four product bundles — Close Management & Analytics, Cash Management, Billing & Revenue, and ERP Replacement (FDP) — composed of roughly twelve named modules; only Agentic Subledgers and App Studio are gated to the ERP Replacement tier. There is no FP&A/forecasting, no AP/procurement, no payroll, no inventory, no tax engine.

## Key Question 2 — System of record or layer on top? Which ERPs? Write-back?

### Takeaway
Numeric is two things at once as of October 2026: (a) in its Close/Cash tiers it is explicitly a layer that reads the GL from NetSuite (transaction-level, every 1-2 minutes) or from Xero/QuickBooks Online/Sage Intacct (trial-balance level), writing journal entries back only to NetSuite and only after human approval; (b) in its ERP Replacement tier (launched 2026-09-22) it claims to be the system of record itself, with the GL being a derived view over stored business events. No support for Workday, SAP, Dynamics, Rillet or Campfire was found anywhere.

### Inferences
- The GL-of-record question therefore has a tiered answer: for Close/Cash/Billing customers the ERP remains the ledger and Numeric is a read-mostly transaction warehouse with a single, approval-gated write path (NetSuite JEs); for FDP customers Numeric claims to be the ledger, and NetSuite becomes an optional downstream posting target during migration.
- Write-back is narrow: only journal entries, only to NetSuite, either in product (cash JEs, batch) or via the MCP/Claude path (`ns_createRecord`). No evidence of writing master data, invoices, or anything to Xero/QBO/Intacct.
- The absence of any Workday/SAP/Dynamics/Oracle Fusion connector, and the "NetSuite replacement" framing, place Numeric squarely in the mid-market/tech-company segment.

## Key Question 3 — Data model: own ledger, audit log, versioning, replay, immutability/provenance

### Takeaway
Numeric claims (vendor text only) a transaction-level warehouse of the ERP GL in the Close tier, and in the FDP tier an event-first model where every business event is stored with its source document and metadata and the debits/credits are a derived view; it claims per-action timestamped audit trails, lineage from number to source, and versioned/logged apps. It makes no public claim of immutability, append-only storage, rule versioning, or deterministic replay of state.

### Cited Findings
- FDP data model: "Instead of compressing reality into journal entries, Numeric stores every business event"; "Numeric ingests all financial activity, retaining all data from the original object"; "A data warehouse, tailored to finance and accounting." — [numeric.io/financial-data-platform](https://www.numeric.io/financial-data-platform)
- Launch release: "stores each business event as a complete record, including the original source document and its metadata. Debits and credits are one view derived from that record, not the record itself." — [Yahoo Finance copy of release, 2026-09-22](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/numeric-launches-financial-data-platform-130000623.html)
- Gilbert on why: "With each change, the journal entries get harder and harder to interpret"; "The objects of accounting don't just happen as one-off events"; "you can't reconstruct that picture from the trail of posted journal entries." — [Accounting Today 2026-09-25](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/numeric-launches-financial-data-platform-to-replace-erps)

### Inferences
- The FDP's "event + derived debits/credits" framing is conceptually close to event sourcing, but nothing published says the event store is append-only, that derivations are versioned, or that state can be rebuilt by replay; "re-upload and the treatment is preserved" implies re-derivation of downstream accounting from a changed input, which is a replay-like property for one record, not a whole-ledger guarantee.
- Audit-trail claims are activity-log claims (who did what, when), not ledger-immutability claims; the vendor's own July 2026 guidance treats immutability as a question to ask vendors, which suggests it is not a headline feature of its own product.
- "Lineage" in Numeric's vocabulary means drill-through from a number to its contract/event/source document, not a formal (event_id, rule_id, rule_version) triple.

### Gaps
- No documentation of storage semantics (append-only, soft-delete, version rows), of how a derived JE is recomputed when a "flow" changes, or of whether historical periods are re-derived or frozen.
- No statement on reproducibility/determinism of FDP accounting derivations; the "deterministic code for calculations" quote refers to the Cash product.
- No SOC report scope, retention policy or audit-log export format was found publicly.

## Key Question 4 — Does Numeric encode business/accounting rules? Who authors them? Versioned?

### Takeaway
Yes, in several places: cash-matching rules (rules engine, AI-assisted creation, AI-suggested from manual matches), JE rules (every JE tied to a rule, preparer/reviewer gate), transaction monitors (user-defined policy checks), recurring-task rules in Prism (sign-off becomes a rule), and in the FDP a rules layer called "flows" plus AI-proposed accounting treatments that the user approves. Authors are users with AI drafting; no public claim of rule versioning or rule lifecycle states was found.
