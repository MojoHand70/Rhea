# Event-Sourced, Deterministic-Replay, and Provenance-First Data Platforms vs. Rhea (as of October 2026)

Scope: survey of systems whose core guarantee is append-only log + deterministic replay + provenance, assessed against Rhea's kernel: (1) append-only event log as system of record; (2) versioned, human-approved, AI-authored rules-as-data executed deterministically; (3) wipe-and-replay reproduces identical state; (4) per-field provenance (event id, rule id, rule version).

## Key Question 1: Event-sourcing infrastructure (EventStoreDB/Kurrent, Axon, Marten, Commanded, Emmett, Rails Event Store) — ERP/accounting backbone? AI-authored-logic story?

### Takeaway
Event-sourcing infrastructure is routinely *justified* by the accounting-ledger analogy and sold into banking/insurance/finance, but none of the major frameworks is marketed as the backbone of an ERP or accounting *product*, and none has an AI-*authored*-logic story — Kurrent's 2026 AI pivot positions the event log as a substrate *for* AI agents (context, audit, observability), not as an executor of AI-written rules.

### Cited Findings
- Event Store rebranded to Kurrent; KurrentDB is pitched as "an event-native data platform" that "improves data analytics, microservices, and AI results by feeding real-time data" — [Kurrent blog](https://www.kurrent.io/blog/event-store-is-evolving-to-kurrent)
- KurrentDB claims to be "the only database purpose-built for event sourcing with native immutability, global ordering, real-time subscriptions, and projections," and provides "integrations for AI agent frameworks with a shared canonical event schema" — [KurrentDB event sourcing page](https://kurrentdb.kurrent.io/event-sourcing/)
- Kurrent's 2026 webinar/event lineup is explicitly agentic: "Event Sourcing: Contextual AI Requires a Contextual Data Model"; "Agentic Observability, Audit & Control with Event-Native Infrastructure" (pitch: event-native infrastructure gives AI teams "complete visibility, auditability, and control over agent deployments from compliance trails to deterministic recovery"); "Two Surfaces, One Substrate" (multi-agent coordination + coding-agent auditability) — [Kurrent events](https://www.kurrent.io/events/)
- A direct inspection of the Kurrent blog (Oct 2026) shows products KurrentDB (v26.2), Capacitor, and Kurrent Cloud, with a dedicated "Agentic AI" use-case category — and **no ERP/accounting positioning anywhere visible**; content centers on event sourcing, microservices, CDC, local-first — [Kurrent blog](https://kurrentdb.kurrent.io/blog/agentic-observability-audit-control-with-event-native-infrastructure)
- Kurrent has long argued event history beats state snapshots as ML/AI input ("How Event Sourcing Can Power Machine Learning") — [Kurrent](https://www.kurrent.io/blog/how-event-sourcing-can-power-machine-learning)
- AxonIQ (Axon Framework + Axon Server) markets "transactional processing": event sourcing as "a single source of truth" whose events "effortlessly meet auditing and compliance needs"; customer sectors are healthcare, banking, insurance, logistics, public sector — i.e., adjacent to but not ERP products; its retail reference architecture *includes* "an accounting system that maintains the journal and books" as one component — [AxonIQ transactional use case](https://www.axoniq.io/use-cases/transactional-processing); [AxonIQ use cases](https://www.axoniq.io/use-cases)
- An academic case study (CLEI 2018) applied event sourcing inside an ERP system and reported it "dramatically increased error traceability" and provided "a natural audit log" — an isolated case study, not a product line — [Vasconsellos, CLEI](https://clei.org/clei2018/docs/SLISW/182931.pdf)
- The accounting-ledger analogy is the canonical motivation for event sourcing: "past entries are never erased or modified; corrections are made by appending new, compensating entries" — [DEV: Event Sourcing and the History of Accounting](https://dev.to/dealeron/event-sourcing-and-the-history-of-accounting-1aah); Fowler notes the event log *can* be the official system of record with databases rebuilt from it at will — [Fowler, Event Sourcing](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html)
- Hobby/demo-scale event-sourced ERPs exist on GitHub (e.g., an "Event Sourcing-based ERP System with a CLI") but nothing production-grade surfaced — [m-maresch/erp-system](https://github.com/m-maresch/erp-system)

### Inferences
- **Kurrent — closest resemblance:** append-only immutable log as system of record, projections as rebuildable read models, and (new in 2025–26) explicit "deterministic recovery / audit / compliance trail for AI agents" language. **Sharpest difference:** the AI story is *agents writing events into the log and being observed through it*; logic executed over the log (projections/subscriptions) is ordinary code written by developers — there is no rules-as-data layer, no approval lifecycle, no per-field provenance stamping, and no ERP domain packaging.
- **Axon — closest resemblance:** event store + replayable projections with audit/compliance framing in financial domains. **Sharpest difference:** rules are compiled Java aggregates/sagas, versioned only as application code; no AI authorship, no runtime-definable logic, no field-level provenance.
- The analogy "event sourcing ≈ double-entry bookkeeping" is ubiquitous as *rhetoric*, yet the frameworks' commercial gravity is fintech transaction processing, not general-ledger/ERP products — the gap Rhea targets (ERP semantics as data on top of the log) appears unoccupied by these vendors.

### Gaps
- No reliable sources found on Marten, Commanded (Elixir), Emmett, or Rails Event Store being used as ERP/accounting product backbones or having any AI-authored-logic story; targeted searching did not surface such positioning, which is itself signal that none exists publicly as of Oct 2026.
- Could not confirm any named commercial ERP built on EventStoreDB/KurrentDB or Axon (vendor case studies name sectors, not ERP products).

## Key Question 2: Deterministic execution platforms (Temporal, Restate, Golem, Rama, Convex, DBOS) — business-rule execution with versioned logic and replay-as-proof?

### Takeaway
Durable-execution platforms make determinism a *recovery* mechanism for developer-written code, not a *proof* mechanism for data-defined business rules: replay exists to resume crashed workflows identically, versioning exists to avoid breaking in-flight executions, and the 2026 wave (Golem, Rama's Agent-o-rama, DBOS) applies this to AI *agents' execution*, never to AI-*authored* rules.

### Cited Findings
- DBOS persists every workflow step's state to Postgres; on crash it "replays from the last committed step: no work is lost, no step is re-executed, no external side effect is duplicated"; the workflow body "must be deterministic: given the same recorded step results, replaying it must take the same path" — [DBOS blog](https://www.dbos.dev/blog/what-is-lightweight-durable-execution)
- Jack Vanlightly's analysis: durable execution frameworks "require determinism in control flow but not in side effects"; non-deterministic operations (clock, random, DB reads used for branching) must be captured as recorded steps — [Demystifying Determinism in Durable Execution](https://daily.dev/posts/demystifying-determinism-in-durable-execution-jack-vanlightly-c2yysfqvx)
- Temporal requires workflow code to be deterministic ("the same Workflow API calls in the same sequence, given the same input") and re-executes code against recorded event history on recovery; mismatches raise nondeterminism errors — [Temporal docs](https://docs.temporal.io/workflow-definition)
- Temporal ships explicit Workflow Versioning/Patching because executions "may live for months or years" and code changes can diverge from recorded history; replayer tests against stored histories are the recommended pre-deploy determinism check — [Temporal Go versioning docs](https://docs.temporal.io/develop/go/versioning); [Using Replay before deploying a new Workflow version](https://patford12.medium.com/using-replay-before-deploying-a-new-workflow-version-with-temporal-3db2d8283956)
- Golem positions itself as "the durable agent runtime": WASM-sandboxed agents with "deterministic durable execution," exactly-once semantics, state that survives failures/updates — "a stateful, potentially long running entity that can never die" — [golem.cloud](https://golem.cloud/); [The Rise of the Agent Runtime](https://golem.cloud/blog/the-rise-of-the-agent-runtime/)
- Rama remains active (v1.9.0 released July 6, 2026) and published a TPC-C benchmark matching CockroachDB at ~40% lower AWS cost; Red Planet Labs open-sourced **Agent-o-rama**, a JVM library for "scalable and stateful LLM agents" with tracing/evals, built on Rama's depots and query topologies — [rama-release-notes](https://github.com/redplanetlabs/rama-release-notes); [Red Planet Labs blog](https://blog.redplanetlabs.com/); [RPL on X](https://x.com/redplanetlabs/status/2003191740480782397)
- 2026 comparison literature treats Temporal, Restate, Inngest, Trigger.dev, and DBOS as one "durable execution engine" category (deterministic replay as the shared mechanism) — [LabHub deep-dive comparison](https://labhub.hopto.org/blog/culture/2026-05-14-temporal-restate-inngest-trigger-dev-dbos-deep-dive-2026?lang=en) (aggregator-grade source; mechanism claims consistent with vendor docs above)
- Research/engineering commentary identifies a "Replay Divergence Problem" for agent runtimes: downstream outputs differ under model-version or prompt changes, "breaking event-sourcing audit assumptions"; proving determinism requires an explicit replay-equivalence test — [Zylos, Replayable Agent Runtimes](https://zylos.ai/research/2026-04-26-replayable-agent-runtimes/)

### Inferences
- **DBOS — closest resemblance:** Postgres as the single durable substrate plus replay-from-log as the core guarantee (structurally the nearest to "Postgres event log + deterministic executor"). **Sharpest difference:** the unit of replay is a *workflow invocation*, not a business event log; logic is Python/TS code, versioned implicitly by deployment, with no rules-as-data, approval lifecycle, or provenance stamping.
- **Temporal — closest resemblance:** replay against recorded history as a first-class correctness test (replayer tests ≈ Rhea's invariant 4, applied to code instead of rules). **Sharpest difference:** determinism is a burden placed on the developer to make recovery safe, not a property the platform proves about business state; versioning (patching) exists to *escape* strict replay, the opposite of Rhea's replay-as-proof.
- **Golem / Agent-o-rama — closest resemblance:** deterministic execution + full trace applied to LLM agents (audit of what the agent did). **Sharpest difference:** the LLM is the *executor* being made durable; in Rhea the LLM is the *author* and is excluded from execution entirely.
- None of these platforms targets "business-rule execution" as data: no platform in this category lets rules be defined at runtime, approved by humans, and versioned as rows rather than deployments.

### Gaps
- No reliable 2025–26 sources found on Restate or Convex specifically marketing versioned business-rule execution or replay-as-proof for business state (Restate appears in category comparisons only; Convex's determinism claims did not surface in searches and are unverified here).

## Key Question 3: Bitemporal/provenance databases (XTDB, Datomic, Dolt, TerminusDB) — ERP/accounting system-of-record positioning? Per-fact provenance? AI layer?

### Takeaway
XTDB is the strongest provenance-first database and explicitly courts audit-heavy financial use cases ("what did we know when a decision was made"), but it provides time-based provenance of *recorded facts*, not rule-based provenance of *derived facts* — no bitemporal database surveyed executes rules at all, and none has an AI-authoring layer.

### Cited Findings
- XTDB is "an immutable SQL database for application development, time-travel reporting and data compliance," bitemporal on every write — [xtdb/xtdb GitHub](https://github.com/xtdb/xtdb); [xtdb.com](https://xtdb.com/)
- XTDB positions bitemporality for audit: valid time lets you "demonstrate what data you actually had available when a decision was made at a specific point in the past," and claims to replace "CDC pipelines, data lake snapshots, custom history tables, and reconstruction scripts" — [XTDB FAQ](https://v1-docs.xtdb.com/resources/faq/); [xtdb.com](https://xtdb.com/)
- XTDB publishes a pattern piece pairing itself with Temporal for "durable, auditable orchestration" — provenance DB + durable execution as complementary layers — [XTDB: Temporal orchestration](https://xtdb.com/discover/temporal-orchestration)
- Datomic is immutable with as-of queries at fact granularity but is not bitemporal; XTDB exposes both time axes as runtime query parameters — [Biff: XTDB compared to other databases](https://biffweb.com/p/xtdb-compared-to-other-databases/)
- Community work explicitly connects temporal databases to event stores, arguing event sourcing needs a third time axis ("tritemporal event sourcing": decision time / valid time / transaction time) for payroll-like business domains — [DEV: Tritemporal Event Sourcing](https://dev.to/giannoudis/tritemporal-event-sourcing-the-missing-time-axis-in-your-event-store-5e24)

### Inferences
- **XTDB — closest resemblance:** immutability + rebuildable indexes + "prove what you knew and when" as the core product promise; the closest commercial articulation of Rhea's auditability-by-replay value, and its audit pitch (decisions defensible to auditors/regulators) overlaps Rhea's statutory-adapter ambitions. **Sharpest difference:** provenance is *temporal* (which fact, which time axis), not *causal* (which event, which rule version produced this field); XTDB stores facts given to it — nothing is derived, so there is nothing like "nothing appears in state without a rule."
- **Datomic — closest resemblance:** accumulate-only facts with full history and as-of reads. **Sharpest difference:** same as XTDB, minus bitemporality; transaction functions are code, not versioned approved data.
- No evidence any of these is positioned as an ERP/accounting system of record (XTDB's financial-services positioning is compliance/audit infrastructure, not ledger or ERP semantics), and none advertises an AI layer beyond generic "feed AI better data" messaging.

### Gaps
- Dolt and TerminusDB did not surface in searches with any ERP/accounting or provenance-of-derivation positioning; no reliable sources found, so they are unassessed rather than ruled out.

## Key Question 4: Ledger infrastructure (TigerBeetle, Formance, Modern Treasury, Fragment) — user-defined or AI-defined rules deriving state from events, with approval and versioning?

### Takeaway
Ledger infrastructure is the only category that already ships "logic as reviewed, versioned data" (Formance's Numscript, Fragment's typed schemas) and, by 2026, an explicit AI stance — but the stance is uniformly "agents propose transactions under human-approved policy," never "agents author the rules"; derivation is fixed double-entry arithmetic, not an extensible rule language.

### Cited Findings
- TigerBeetle builds double-entry accounting into the database itself: accounts and transfers as primitives, balance-tracking and invariants (e.g., balance limits) "enforced within the database," applications kept stateless, ~10K transfers processed per DB query — [TigerBeetle DESIGN.md](https://github.com/tigerbeetle/tigerbeetle/blob/main/docs/DESIGN.md); [TigerBeetle docs](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/single-page/); [tigerbeetle.com](https://tigerbeetle.com/)
- Formance's Numscript is a DSL for money movement — "human-readable and machine-safe" — now a standalone open-source interpreter (Go, MIT, formal grammar, CLI, LSP, playground); the recommended practice is "one reviewed, versioned script per business event: settle, refund, payout, write-off," readable by product, finance, and auditors — [Numscript platform page](https://www.formance.com/platform/numscript); [Formance Numscript blog](https://www.formance.com/blog/engineering/numscript)
- Formance v3 adds versioned Schemas: chart-of-accounts-structured validation rules for transactions/accounts, with strict or audit-mode enforcement — [Formance v3 changelog](https://docs-archive.formance.com/v3.x-changelog)
- Formance's 2026 agentic position paper: "Block agent write access at the tool surface so the model never holds a credential that can change a balance"; a policy layer outside the model runs budget/allowlist checks, compiles commands into Numscript, and "applies the threshold that decides whether the command posts now or waits for a signed human approval"; every agent proposal is stamped with agent ID, model version (e.g. "planner-2026.08.2"), mandate ID, and idempotency key — [Formance: AI Payments, Where Ledgers Fit in the Agentic Stack](https://www.formance.com/blog/embedded-finance/ai-payments-agentic-ledger-stack)
- Fragment is "the ledger API for engineers who move money," now marketed as "gives your coding agent financial superpowers": a GraphQL API where funds flows are modeled as "declarative, typed schemas," the Schema API "define[s] and version[s] the ledger Schema," and entries are immutable, balanced, idempotent-by-key — [fragment.dev](https://fragment.dev/); [api-evangelist/fragment profile](https://github.com/api-evangelist/fragment); [Fragment: A Ledger for Engineers](https://fragment.dev/blog/a-ledger-for-engineers)
- The "agents propose, humans approve, [system] enforces" pattern is becoming an industry idiom beyond fintech ledgers — Ledger (hardware wallet) launched "Agent Stack" in July 2026 under exactly that slogan, with configurable Agent Rules and mandatory human confirmation for sensitive actions — [Ledger Agent Stack](https://shop.ledger.com/pages/ledger-agent-stack); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/07/16/ledger-launches-agent-stack-keep-ai-agents-away-crypto-keys/)
- Incumbent ERP is adding agent layers to the GL: Oracle Fusion 26B ships a "Ledger Agent" for an "agentic AI-powered general ledger experience" — [Oracle Fusion 26B release notes](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/readiness/erp/26b/fins26b/26B-fin-wn-f43814.htm)
- Modern Treasury Ledgers is an immutable double-entry ledger service — [Modern Treasury Ledgers](https://www.moderntreasury.com/products/ledgers)

### Inferences
- **Formance — the single closest commercial analog to Rhea's rule layer:** versioned, human-reviewed scripts-as-data (Numscript) executed deterministically against an immutable ledger, plus a published human-approval-threshold pattern for AI-originated commands and per-transaction provenance metadata (agent/model/mandate IDs). **Sharpest difference:** the AI *proposes transactions* (instances), humans author the scripts (rules); Numscript's domain is postings between accounts, not general object derivation; and there is no wipe-and-replay determinism proof — the ledger is the record, not a projection.
- **Fragment — closest resemblance:** declarative versioned schemas constraining what entries are legal; 2026 marketing explicitly targets *coding agents* as the schema/integration authors (AI writes the integration under type-checked constraints). **Sharpest difference:** no rule execution over events — the application decides what to post; the ledger validates balance, nothing more.
- **TigerBeetle — closest resemblance:** invariants enforced in the kernel, state derivable only through fixed primitives (nothing appears without a transfer ≈ nothing appears without a rule). **Sharpest difference:** the rule set is frozen at compile time (double-entry arithmetic); deliberately *not* extensible at runtime — the exact opposite trade from Rhea.
- Across the category, "versioning + approval" applies to *scripts and policies written by humans*; AI involvement stops at proposing transactions within those policies. No ledger vendor lets AI author the derivation rules themselves.

### Gaps
- No sources found detailing Modern Treasury's own rule/approval/versioning model or any Modern Treasury AI-rules offering (searches surfaced the unrelated Ledger hardware company instead); its Ledgers product page confirms only the immutable double-entry service.

## Key Question 5: Is anyone publicly combining event sourcing + LLM-authored declarative logic + human approval in one system?

### Takeaway
No shipped system found combining all of Rhea's four properties; the nearest neighbors are (a) 2026 agent-runtime research (ESAA, ActiveGraph) that has the append-only-log/deterministic-replay/lineage kernel but applies it to *agent execution* rather than business rules, and (b) AI-native ERP startups (Rillet, Campfire, DualEntry, Digits) that have AI + human approval + audit trails in the ERP domain but on conventional mutable-state architectures without rules-as-data or replay-as-proof.

### Cited Findings
- **ESAA** ("Event Sourcing for Autonomous Agents," 2026) is the closest architectural statement: agents "emit structured intentions while a deterministic orchestrator validates, persists events in an append-only log, and projects a verifiable materialized view"; it explicitly criticizes existing agent frameworks for mutable snapshot state, "no native mechanism for deterministic replay of the complete decision trail," and unbounded blast radius of a compromised agent — [ESAA, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/401278725_ESAA_Event_Sourcing_for_Autonomous_Agents_in_LLM-Based_Software_Engineering)
- **ActiveGraph** ("The Log is the Agent," Yohei Nakajima, arXiv May 2026, Apache-2.0 implementation): "append-only event log [is] the source of truth" with the working graph "a deterministic projection of that log"; guarantees deterministic replay of any run, cheap forking at any event, and "end-to-end lineage from a high-level goal down to the individual model call that produced each artifact"; replay determinism is achieved by recording model/tool responses content-addressed by request hash — [arXiv:2605.21997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21997)
- The ESAA/ActiveGraph pattern is being popularized as "event sourcing comes to AI systems" — [Developers Digest](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/log-is-the-agent-event-sourced-ai)
- The AI-native ERP cohort (Rillet — $70M Series B; Campfire — multi-entity consolidation, rev-rec, full GL; DualEntry — claims 90% of manual tasks removed; plus Numeric, Puzzle, Digits) is defined by AI agents acting inside accounting workflows — [Rillet, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rillet); [DualEntry](https://www.dualentry.com/); [Priority: What is AI-native ERP](https://www.priority-software.com/resources/ai-native-erp/)
- Buyer-side criteria for "AI-native ERP" now include Rhea-like traceability — "when an AI agent proposes a journal entry... the ERP records which data was used, which model or policy was applied, what action was proposed, and who approved it" — but as an audit-trail requirement, not a deterministic-replay kernel — [erpclaw.ai AI-Native ERP 5-trait test](https://www.erpclaw.ai/ai-native-erp/)
- The decision-automation community (IBM lineage) describes the half-step: "LLM-extracted rules, once reviewed by a human, can automate decisions with traceability and determinism" — i.e., LLM authors rules, human approves, deterministic rule engine executes — as an *approach*, demonstrated with conventional BRMS/decision engines, not an event-sourced product — [Feillet, Medium](https://medium.com/@pierrefeillet/approaches-in-using-generative-ai-for-business-automation-the-path-to-comprehensive-decision-3dd91c57e38f)
- Human-in-the-loop interception of LLM outputs (risk-scored review queues before execution) is standard guidance in agent-orchestration tooling — [Orkes: Human-in-the-Loop in Agentic Workflows](https://orkes.io/blog/human-in-the-loop/)
- Kurrent markets the substrate for exactly this combination ("compliance trails to deterministic recovery" for agent deployments) without shipping the rule layer — [Kurrent events](https://www.kurrent.io/events/)
- Formance ships the approval-gate half in production fintech: AI proposes, policy layer + signed human approval gates posting, provenance metadata stamped per transaction — but humans write the Numscript rules — [Formance agentic-ledger blog](https://www.formance.com/blog/embedded-finance/ai-payments-agentic-ledger-stack)
- A known failure mode validates Rhea's "model output is data, never executed" invariant: replayable-agent-runtime research names the Replay Divergence Problem — if the LLM sits in the execution path, model-version or prompt changes break event-sourcing audit assumptions — [Zylos](https://zylos.ai/research/2026-04-26-replayable-agent-runtimes/)

### Inferences
- The three ingredients each exist in production somewhere — append-only log + deterministic replay (Kurrent/Axon/DBOS/Temporal), versioned reviewed logic-as-data (Formance Numscript, Fragment schemas), AI-drafts-human-approves (Formance, Ledger Agent Stack, AI-native ERPs, decision-automation practice) — but as of October 2026 no public system composes all three with per-field rule provenance in a business/ERP domain. Rhea's specific composition appears to be unoccupied.
- The field splits cleanly on *where the LLM sits*: agent runtimes (Golem, Agent-o-rama, ESAA, ActiveGraph) make the LLM a durable *executor* and then fight the Replay Divergence Problem; Rhea's design (LLM authors rules offline; only versioned approved rules execute) sidesteps that problem by construction — the deterministic kernel never contains a model call. ESAA's "structured intentions validated by a deterministic orchestrator" is the closest published articulation of this same separation, at the level of agent actions rather than durable business rules.
- AI-native ERP competitors converge on Rhea's *audit-trail* language (what data, what policy, what action, who approved) without the mechanism that makes it checkable (wipe projections, replay, compare) — a differentiator that is demonstrable rather than claimable.

### Gaps
- Could not verify architecture internals of Rillet/Campfire/DualEntry/Digits (no public engineering posts found describing their state models); the claim that they are *not* event-sourced replay kernels is an absence of evidence, not a documented fact.
- No 2025–26 QCon/DDD-Europe conference talk specifically on "event sourcing + LLM-authored rules + human approval" surfaced in searches; no Hacker News thread specifically on that combination was found (general ES-for-accounting threads exist but predate the AI-authoring angle).
- ESAA's publication venue/peer-review status unverified (found via ResearchGate listing only).
