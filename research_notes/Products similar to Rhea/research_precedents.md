# Research-World Precedents for Rhea (AI-authored declarative ERP logic, deterministic kernel, REA ancestry)

Survey date: 2026-10-05. Rhea's four pillars used as the comparison frame: (P1) AI-authored declarative logic as versioned data, (P2) human-approval lifecycle before activation, (P3) deterministic execution/replay over an append-only event log, (P4) per-field provenance. Items combining 3+ pillars are flagged **[3+ PILLARS]**.

## Q1: REA (McCarthy 1982) — implementations, modern revivals, REA + LLMs

### Takeaway
REA is alive as an ontology standard (ISO/IEC 15944-4) and as a commons/P2P-economy revival (Valueflows, hREA on Holochain), but no source was found combining REA with LLMs, and no commercial ERP startup citing REA surfaced. Rhea's REA lineage appears uncontested territory on the AI side.

### Cited Findings
- REA was proposed in 1982 by William E. McCarthy as a generalized accounting model built on resources, events, and agents; it starts from the economic event and links it to resources and agents, with accounting entries generated later and automatically from that data — debits/credits/ledgers "are not necessary in such a model" — [Wikipedia: Resources, Events, Agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resources,_Events,_Agents)
- ISO/IEC 15944-4:2015 ("Business transaction scenarios — Accounting and economic ontology") standardizes the Open-edi Business Transaction Ontology (OeBTO), covering a business-transaction state machine and a repository for business rules; the ISO Open-EDI workgroup adopted the REA ontology — [ISO/IEC 15944-4:2015](https://www.iso.org/standard/67199.html); McCarthy's own ISO 15944-4 presentation is hosted by Valueflows — [REA Ontology ISO-15944-4 (McCarthy, 2013)](https://www.valueflo.ws/linked-docs/REA-Ontology_ISO-15944-4--BillMcCarthy_20131107.pdf)
  - Closest resemblance to Rhea: REA/OeBTO is literally Rhea's "language of ERP" ancestor — economic events as primary records, state derived from them, plus a declared business-rules repository. Sharpest difference: it is a modeling ontology/standard, with no execution kernel, no AI authorship, no replay semantics.
- Valueflows is a vocabulary/ontology explicitly built as an extension of REA for networked economic activity (supply chains, circular economies, community ecosystems) — [valueflo.ws](https://www.valueflo.ws/)
- hREA implements the full Valueflows specification as modular Holochain building blocks — "REA economic network coordination software" for supply chain, project coordination, and resource planning — [hrea.io](https://hrea.io/); [github.com/h-REA](https://github.com/h-REA)
  - Closest resemblance: REA events as the system of record in a running implementation; append-only by Holochain's DHT nature. Sharpest difference: decentralized commons-economy coordination, no AI rule authorship, no rules-as-data layer, no human-approval lifecycle.
- Sensorica's True Commons stacks REA + Valueflows + hREA + Holochain as "distributed multi-agent systems with reflexivity, learning, and local memory" for peer production — [github.com/Sensorica/true_commons](https://github.com/Sensorica/true_commons)
- Academic line connecting REA to shared-ledger/triple-entry accounting and blockchain: "REA, Triple-Entry Accounting and Blockchain: Converging Paths to Shared Ledger Systems" — [arXiv 2005.07802](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.07802)
- Ontology-engineering follow-on: "A Core Ontology for Economic Exchanges" (UFO-based rethinking of economic exchange, citing REA tradition) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-62522-1_27); REA Business Management Ontology work continues in the conceptual-modeling community — [CEUR Vol-1612 paper 6](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-1612/paper6.pdf)
- A targeted search for "REA accounting model LLM" returned only classic REA material; the search explicitly surfaced no REA+LLM work — [search context: Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resources,_Events,_Agents), [O'Reilly AIS appendix](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/accounting-information-systems/9781118162309/c02-28.html)

### Inferences
- The REA revival is concentrated in the P2P/commons world (Valueflows/hREA), not in ERP vendors or AI research. Rhea's claim "REA + LLM authorship + deterministic kernel" has no direct precedent in anything found.
- ISO 15944-4's "repository for business rules" inside OeBTO is the closest standards-level anticipation of Rhea's rules-as-data, but it was never paired with a generative author.

### Gaps
- No reliable source found for any startup or product marketing itself on REA (hREA is a grant/collective-funded open-source project, not a startup in the usual sense). Could not confirm or deny quiet REA usage inside commercial ledgers.
- No source found combining REA with LLMs in any form (paper, prototype, or product).

## Q2: LLM-generates-DSL research 2023–2026 (declarative specs executed by deterministic engines, HITL; accounting/tax/enterprise applications)

### Takeaway
A dense 2023–2026 literature exists on LLMs emitting declarative artifacts — Catala tax code, DMN decision models, Datalog, Rego policy — executed by deterministic symbolic engines, with human-in-the-loop repeatedly named best practice. All of it is generation-plus-validation; none of it adds a versioned approval lifecycle, an event log, or replay.

### Cited Findings
- **Catala tax-law generation [strongest tax/accounting match]**: "Translating Tax Law to Code with LLMs: A Benchmark and Evaluation Framework" (Gabriele Lorenzo, Aldo Pietromatera, Nils Holzenberger), NLLP Workshop 2025 (Suzhou, Nov 2025), pp. 31–47 — builds a benchmark + training dataset for generating Catala code from legal text, with an evaluation framework extending code-generation metrics; experiments with few-shot and fine-tuned models "suggest the feasibility of automating legal code generation," contrasting with prior formal-representation attempts — [ACL Anthology 2025.nllp-1.4](https://aclanthology.org/2025.nllp-1.4/)
  - Closest resemblance: LLM authors declarative tax logic for a deterministic law-execution language — Rhea pillar P1 applied to exactly Rhea's statutory-adapter domain. Sharpest difference: an offline translation benchmark; no running system, no approval lifecycle, no event log, no provenance machinery.
- **DMN from legal text**: "From Legal Text to Executable Decision Models: Evaluating Structured Representations for Legal Decision Model Generation" (accepted ICAIL 2026) — LLMs generate executable decision models from the Dutch Environment and Planning Act; adding input/output constraints to prompts improved similarity 37–54% over raw-text baseline; generated models matched gold-standard outcomes on 51–53% of test scenarios; built on 95 production-grade decision models, released publicly — [arXiv 2604.17153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17153). Related search summary of the same line recommends a workflow where "domain experts define input/output specifications, LLMs generate initial decision models, and experts verify and refine" — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.17153)
  - Closest resemblance: production-grade government decision logic drafted by LLM, verified by experts (P1 + informal P2). Sharpest difference: evaluation study, not a governed system; no versioning, no replay.
- **DMN as LLM guardrail (inverted direction)**: "DMN-Guided Prompting: A Framework for Controlling LLM Behavior" — uses DMN decision tables to structure LLM behavior, deployed in a graduate course "while having the instructor in the loop" — [arXiv 2505.11701](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.11701v2)
- **Datalog**: LogicLoc has an LLM synthesize Datalog queries executed by the Soufflé engine with a neuro-symbolic feedback loop — [arXiv 2604.16021](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16021v2); VADAOrchestra has an LLM plan workflows as Datalog+/- programs evaluated by a symbolic engine, a separation that "lets the LLM handle planning flexibility while the symbolic component... guarantees determinism" — [Pith summary of arXiv 2606.22485](https://pith.science/paper/2606.22485) (aggregator summary; primary PDF not independently verified)
  - Closest resemblance: the exact "model output is data, a deterministic engine executes" split of Rhea invariant 3. Sharpest difference: ephemeral queries/plans, not versioned business rules; no human approval, no event log.
- **Policy-as-code (Rego/OPA)**: ARPaCCino, an agentic-RAG system, "generates formal Rego rules from natural language descriptions... then verifies and applies them" for Infrastructure-as-Code compliance — [arXiv 2507.10584](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10584v1); Red Hat's emerging-tech work frames hand-written Rego as a "Rego tax" and uses LLM orchestrators to generate context-aware OPA Gatekeeper policies from live clusters (March 2026) — [Red Hat Emerging Technologies](https://next.redhat.com/2026/03/20/eliminating-the-rego-tax-how-ai-orchestrators-automate-kubernetes-compliance/)
  - Closest resemblance: ARPaCCino chains LLM authorship → automated verification → deterministic policy evaluation (P1 + P3-lite). Sharpest difference: verification is automated, not a human approval lifecycle; domain is infrastructure, not business state; no event log or provenance.
- **Field synthesis**: the "LLM-Assisted Rule-Based Development" topic survey states current best practice is "a hybridized, human-in-the-loop development model" because full automation fails on high logical interdependency — [EmergentMind](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/llm-assisted-rule-based-development); HILDE studies "intentional code generation" with human-in-the-loop decision surfacing — [arXiv 2505.22906](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22906); compliance-domain variant "Code-as-Auditor: Executable Compliance Reasoning via Regulation-to-Code" — [arXiv 2609.19199](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19199); regulator-side application: "Accelerating Radio Spectrum Regulation Workflows with LLMs" — [arXiv 2403.17819](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.17819)

### Inferences
- The LLM→DSL→deterministic-engine pattern is now a recognized architecture ("decouple candidate proposal from validation; delegate deterministic enforcement to symbolic engines" per the neuro-symbolic verification literature, [arXiv 2605.26942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26942)). Rhea's invariant 3 matches the field consensus exactly.
- What the literature treats as a one-shot pipeline (generate → validate → use), Rhea treats as a governed lifecycle (draft → approved → active → superseded) over an event-sourced store. No found paper models rule *versions* as first-class data with activation events.

### Gaps
- No paper found applying LLM-authored declarative rules to operational accounting/bookkeeping (journal derivation, VAT computation on live transactions) as opposed to statutory tax-law translation. The Catala benchmark is the nearest neighbor.
- The VADAOrchestra citation rests on an aggregator summary; the primary paper was not fetched.

## Q3: Rules as Code / computational law — government logic as data; LLM authorship with approval?

### Takeaway
Rules as Code supplies Rhea's "deterministic engine executing law-as-data" half (OpenFisca, Catala, Blawx), and since 2023 the community has actively tested LLM authorship — always concluding humans must validate. Jason Morris's Blawx + GPT-4 line is the closest thing found to Rhea's full authoring loop, including explanation/provenance via symbolic justifications. **[3+ PILLARS]** for the Blawx/PHAC experiment.

### Cited Findings
- Catala: "a programming language for the law" formalizing legal computations (Merigoux et al., Inria), designed so code sits beside statute text; the French family-benefits implementation is ~1,500 lines including the law's text — [arXiv 2103.03198](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.03198)
  - Closest resemblance: deterministic, auditable law-to-code with text-code pairing (provenance to the statute). Sharpest difference: rules are code written by lawyer-programmer pairs, not data drafted by AI; no event log; versioning is git, not an in-system lifecycle.
- OpenFisca: "lightweight, modular and scalable Python-based rules engine" turning legislation into executable tax/benefit models, promoted as a digital commons by the French state and profiled by OECD OPSI — [OpenFisca docs](https://openfisca.readthedocs.io/en/latest/); [OECD OPSI whitepaper attachment](https://oecd-opsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Whitepaper-Attachment-02-About-OpenFisca.pdf); [Labo Société Numérique profile](https://labo.societenumerique.gouv.fr/en/articles/openfisca-quand-un-commun-numerique-transforme-la-loi-en-code/)
- LLM × RaC synthesis (Feb 2025): LLMs can "identify and extract applicable rules... codify these rules... generate test cases," but in testing "LLMs produced incorrect output on complex rules by missing important elements or making unwarranted assumptions" — [Law School Policy Review: Rules as Code and Large Language Models](https://lawschoolpolicyreview.com/2025/02/28/rules-as-code-and-large-language-models/)
- Jason Morris (Blawx author) tested whether GPT-4 could generate Blawx encodings of legal texts, "potentially shortcutting the encoding of laws and allowing more human labour to focus on editing and validating those encodings" (April 2023) — [GPT4 and Rules as Code](https://gauntlet173.github.io/post/2023_04_12_llm_and_rac/); Blawx itself is "a user-friendly web-based tool for Rules as Code" with symbolic execution — [github.com/Lexpedite/blawx](https://github.com/Lexpedite/blawx)
- **[3+ PILLARS]** The Public Health Agency of Canada experiment (privacy_rac_demo) pairs a human-validated Blawx encoding of part of the Privacy Act with a generative-AI chatbot, so answers are "trustworthy, with transparency that goes into the actual reasoning, linked to the text of the law itself" — [github.com/PHACDataHub/privacy_rac_demo](https://github.com/PHACDataHub/privacy_rac_demo) (via [Morris's LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jason-morris-09684023_github-phacdatahubprivacyracdemo-an-activity-7107807753884090368-dQuW))
  - Pillars present: AI-assisted authorship of declarative encodings (P1, in Morris's GPT-4 experiments), human editing/validation before use (P2, informal), deterministic symbolic execution with reasoning traces linked to source text (P3-execution + P4-style provenance). Sharpest difference vs Rhea: the domain is legal Q&A, not business state; there is no append-only business event log, no replay, and the approval step is craft practice, not a recorded lifecycle with activation events.
- Comparative study: "Rules as Code vs. ChatGPT: Lessons from Converting..." (Perron & Logie, Dec 2024, HyperModeLex/Univ. Bologna) contrasts hand-encoded RaC with ChatGPT answers on the same rules — [PDF](https://site.unibo.it/hypermodelex/en/publications/2024-12-perron-logie.pdf/@@download/file/2024-12-PERRON-LOGIE.pdf)
- A "Rules as Code" syntax/machine-readable-legislation survey predating LLM wave: "The Legislative Recipe: Syntax for Machine-Readable Legislation" — [arXiv 2108.08678](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.08678)

### Inferences
- The RaC community converged on exactly Rhea's division of labor — AI drafts, human validates, deterministic engine executes — but delivers it as artisan workflow around git repos, not as a kernel with a draft→approved→active state machine recorded as events. Rhea mechanizes what RaC does socially.
- Catala's statute-text/code pairing is the field's strongest provenance precedent; it is provenance to *source law*, whereas Rhea's is provenance of *derived facts* to (event, rule, version).

### Gaps
- No RaC project found that stores rules as versioned database rows with an in-system approval activity; all found projects version via code repositories.
- OpenFisca + LLM authorship specifically: the NLLP-adjacent literature targets Catala; no OpenFisca-specific LLM-authoring paper was confirmed.

## Q4: BPM research — generative/AI-augmented BPM, process mining × LLMs, declarative process languages

### Takeaway
BPM is the research community whose vision documents sound most like Rhea: the 2023 ABPMS manifesto ("AI within a set of restrictions"), the April 2026 Agentic BPM manifesto ("framed autonomy" with normative frames, escalation, auditability), and DCR Graphs (declarative, runtime-adaptable workflow-as-data used in Danish government). LLM generation of declarative process models exists (Terpsichora, 2025) but with automated — not human — validation.

### Cited Findings
- "AI-Augmented Business Process Management Systems: A Research Manifesto" (Dumas + 13 co-authors, ACM TMIS 2023): defines ABPMS as process-aware systems using "trustworthy AI technology to reason and act upon data, within a set of restrictions, with the aim to continuously adapt and improve a set of business processes" — [arXiv 2201.12855](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12855); [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3576047)
  - Closest resemblance: AI-driven continuous adaptation under explicit restrictions, explainability as a core requirement. Sharpest difference: a research agenda, not a system; adaptation targets process execution, not versioned rule authorship; no event-log replay determinism.
- "Agentic Business Process Management: A Research Manifesto" (20+ authors incl. Bozen-Bolzano, Sapienza, Oxford, IBM Research; April 2026): proposes "framed autonomy" — agents that "perceive, reason, and choose how to act within a process frame"; distinguishes **normative frames** (obligations/prohibitions/permissions) from **operational frames** (executable specifications); mandates escalation to humans at low confidence and "accountability and traceability attributes (trace logs, auditability)" — [arXiv 2603.18916](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18916v3); [ScienceDirect version](https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0306437926000529)
  - Closest resemblance: the governance architecture — declared frames as data, human escalation, audit traces — is Rhea's invariants 2 and 5 restated as a research program. Sharpest difference (and the deep one): APM puts the *agent inside the runtime*, choosing actions; Rhea confines the agent to *authoring rules offline*, with a deterministic kernel as the only actor. Rhea trades autonomy for replayability; APM accepts non-determinism and compensates with traceability.
- **Terpsichora** (Santos, Coutinho, Baião, Spyrides, Lopes; *Process Science* vol. 2, art. 21, Springer 2025): LLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.0 Flash) generate MP-Declare declarative process models via role-play prompting, knowledge injection, few-shot examples, and two constrained-generation modes (function calling vs JSON-schema structured output); a Pydantic metamodel enforces structural integrity and a Declare4Py validation module triggers regeneration on failure; 2,000 synthetic models evaluated; the paper "does not explicitly address human approval or verification workflows" and has "no explicit discussion of versioning... or runtime adaptation" — [Springer, Process Science](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44311-025-00029-1)
  - Closest resemblance: LLM emits strict, schema-validated declarative models as JSON — nearly Rhea's agent contract (invariant 3) verbatim. Sharpest difference: purpose is synthetic dataset generation for research, not live execution; validation is automated, no human gate; no lifecycle, log, or provenance.
- DCR Graphs: declarative process notation where "process logic is specified by a set of constraints rather than by explicitly defining all possible execution sequences," enabling high runtime flexibility; commercialized by DCR Solutions with declarative process mining offerings — [DCR Solutions blog](https://dcrsolutions.net/blog/declarative-process-mining/); DCR tooling and research base at DTU — [DCR Workbench chapter](https://www.riverpublishers.com/pdf/ebook/chapter/RP_9788793519817C5.pdf); open-source declarative modeling editor — [DTU Research Database](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/an-open-source-modeling-editor-for-declarative-process-models/)
  - Closest resemblance: workflow logic held as adaptable declarative data executed by an engine — the nearest *production* analogue to Rhea's rules-as-data kernel (used in Danish government casework per DCR Solutions' positioning). Sharpest difference: human modelers author DCR graphs; no LLM authorship with an approval lifecycle was found in DCR literature as of this survey.
- Pre-LLM NLP→declarative-model work exists ("Assisted Declarative Process Creation from Natural Language," IEEE 2019) — [IEEE Xplore 8907309](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/8907309); DECLARE itself dates to Pesic & van der Aalst's mid-2000s work, with ongoing discovery research — [arXiv 2005.10085](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.10085)
- DCR graphs are also used to formalize smart-contract design patterns (deterministic contract logic) — [Springer SoSyM 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10270-026-01366-w)

### Inferences
- The BPM field has each Rhea ingredient in a different project — declarative models as runtime data (DCR), LLM generation with schema validation (Terpsichora), governance frames and auditability (Agentic BPM manifesto) — but no single system was found uniting them.
- The Agentic BPM manifesto is the best "conceptual framework that anticipates Rhea" for positioning purposes; Rhea can be described as taking its framed-autonomy governance and pushing autonomy entirely out of the runtime into authoring.

### Gaps
- Process mining × LLM commercial work (e.g., Celonis AI capabilities) was not covered by a dedicated search in this pass; no citable source gathered.
- No paper found on LLM-authored DCR graphs specifically (Terpsichora targets MP-Declare); absence here reflects my searches, not a verified absence in the literature.

## Q5: Self-adaptive / self-evolving software — governed, versioned, declarative self-modification

### Takeaway
Self-evolving LLM-agent research (2025–2026) is booming but evolves prompts, memory, tools, and workflows through automated feedback loops — the opposite of Rhea's governed, human-approved, versioned rule changes. The field's own risk literature ("misevolution") effectively argues for Rhea-style governance without building it.

### Cited Findings
- "A Comprehensive Survey of Self-Evolving AI Agents: A New Paradigm Bridging Foundation Models and Lifelong Agentic Systems" (2025): most agent systems "rely on manually crafted configurations that remain static after deployment"; evolution techniques automatically enhance agents from interaction data and environmental feedback, targeting "foundation models, agent prompts, memory, tools, workflows, and communication mechanisms" via a loop of system inputs, agent system, environment, and optimisers — [arXiv 2508.07407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07407)
  - Closest resemblance: the ambition — a system whose behavior changes at runtime without redeploying code. Sharpest difference: changes are continuous, automatic, and unversioned; no approval gate, no determinism, no replay.
- Companion survey: "A Survey of Self-Evolving Agents: What, When, How, and Where to Evolve" — [arXiv 2507.21046](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.21046)
- Risk literature: "Your Agent May Misevolve: Emergent Risks in Self-evolving LLM Agents" documents emergent failure modes when agents modify themselves — [arXiv 2509.26354 via HF Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.26354); "Do Self-Evolving Agents Forget?" shows self-evolution is "often non-monotonic, with adapting to new task distributions progressively degrading previously acquired capabilities" — [arXiv 2605.09315](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09315)
- Human-gated variant exists in evaluation form: "Agent Gym: A Framework for Continuous Evaluation and Evolution of LLM Agents Through Human-in-the-Loop Feedback" — [arXiv 2608.15591](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.15591)
- Neuro-symbolic verification of LLM outputs for data-sensitive domains formalizes the generate-then-verify split: "formal verification decouples candidate proposal from claim validation... delegating deterministic enforcement to symbolic solvers" — [arXiv 2605.26942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26942)

### Inferences
- No found self-evolving system expresses behavior change as *versioned declarative data with a human approval lifecycle*; the misevolution/forgetting results are the empirical case for Rhea's design (append-only versions make degradation diagnosable and reversible; approval gates block misevolution).
- Rhea occupies a quadrant the surveys' own taxonomy leaves empty: evolution target = declarative rule base; optimiser = LLM + human approval; guarantee = deterministic replay.

### Gaps
- No dedicated search was run for event-sourcing/CQRS + LLM-authored logic; no work on deterministic replay of AI-authored rules over append-only logs surfaced in any adjacent search either. I found no reliable source on anyone combining replay determinism (P3) with AI authorship (P1) — this appears to be Rhea's most defensible novelty, but absence-of-evidence here is partly a search-coverage limit.
- Across all five questions, **no system was found combining all four Rhea pillars**; the closest multi-pillar items are the Blawx/PHAC experiment (Q3, ~3 pillars, informal) and ARPaCCino (Q2, P1 + deterministic evaluation, no human gate).
